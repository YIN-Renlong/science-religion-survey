    Methods, sources & limitationsHow to read these figures and return to the research

    What this represents

    Theos and The Faraday Institute for Science and Religion commissioned a YouGov online survey of 5,153 UK adults. Fieldwork ran from 5 May to 13 June 2021. The report by Nick Spencer and Hannah Waite was published in 2022.

    The source describes the percentages as weighted to represent UK adults aged 18+. The full report describes recruitment through the YouGov panel and sample quotas. This page reproduces the published weighted percentages and, where provided, both the actual respondent count (unweighted n) and the weighted base.

    There are 75 main table blocks and 16 blocks in the generational appendix, including repeated questions and three derived measures. These are 91 table records, not 91 distinct survey questions. The supplied tables do not contain every questionnaire item or respondent-level data.

    Expert interviews belong to the wider research, but are not visualised as a representative survey here. The data describe opinions; they do not settle scientific or theological questions. The ten main figures select comparisons about compatibility, particular sciences, wording, uncertainty, confidence, scientific limits, evolution, explanatory limits, ethics and religion. Age, technology, qualifications and all other published topics remain in the reference archive. Direct source links open only the relevant record; the main presentation stays at ten figures.

    How to read the charts

    • Every value reproduces a published percentage. Combined “net” rows come directly from the tables; they are not calculated by adding rounded categories.
    • Compatibility scores 1–5 are grouped as incompatible, 6–10 as compatible. There is no neutral midpoint on the 1–10 scale. The detailed charts show all scores separately.
    • Dot and lollipop plots share a 0–100% axis. Heatmap colour uses a labelled 0–50% scale. Range bands span eight question values, not confidence intervals. Waffle squares each represent one percentage point, not one respondent. Values are never rescaled to add to 100. Multiple-response questions in the archive use separate bars.
    • “Don’t know”, neutral, “not sure” and “I don’t know what this is” retain their distinct source meanings.
    • A dash is preserved as —. Its meaning is not inferred as zero. Published 0% remains 0%.
    • Numerical reading notes describe the displayed values. The labelled course interpretations are editorial explanations and discussion prompts, not extra findings or quotations attributed to the source authors. Highlighted responses are the largest at the published precision; equal rounded values can produce ties. The main comparisons use each question’s total; no respondent-level data are combined. All group values are retained in the optional supporting tables.
    • No significance tests, causal claims, modelled values or confidence intervals are added. Percentage-point gaps are simple differences between the displayed rounded percentages.

    Groups & small samples

    The main tables provide gender, age, ethnicity and region separately. These are not joint filters: “Male” and “16–29” cannot be combined into a new subgroup using these aggregate tables.

    A question about Christianity does not identify a Christian respondent. Religious affiliation is available as a question outcome, not as a general grouping dimension in the supplied tables.

    The supporting tables flag unweighted bases below 100 as a project caution convention, not a publisher-defined significance threshold. Very small groups may yield unstable percentages. Generational bases are not supplied, so their absence is shown explicitly.

    Age and generational differences describe this 2021 sample. They do not establish individual change, trends over time or separate age and cohort effects.

    Source inconsistencies retained

    • The stated population is 18+, but printed age labels include 16–29 and Gen Z 16–24. Labels are retained; no under-18 participation is inferred.
    • The main tables and generational appendix sometimes report different total nets. For “Science will be able to explain everything one day”, agreement is 29% in the main table and 30% in the appendix. Both remain separate.
    • Stem cell research is printed as q11_2 in the tables, duplicating nuclear power. The questionnaire identifies it as q11_1. Both identifiers remain in the exported data.
    • The q8d statements concern being an atheist, while the source base label says “Those answering about faith”. This discrepancy is disclosed on each relevant record.
    • Thresholds for the appendix’s low/medium/high knowledge and confidence scores are not specified in the supplied tables.
